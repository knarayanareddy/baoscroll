# The Lighthouse That Remembered Names — cinematic production plan

## North-star test

At each scroll position, the reader must be able to answer four questions without reading UI copy:

1. **Where is the keeper?**
2. **What is the keeper trying to do right now?**
3. **What resists or responds to that action?**
4. **What has physically changed because of it?**

If a chapter only changes color, shows a scenic plate, or moves a camera through empty props, it fails.

---

## The story grammar (borrow the method, not Bao's content)

Bao works because each chapter is a short, authored film shot built from the same reversible contract:

```text
chapter-local scroll progress
→ keeper blocking and pose
→ camera path / FOV / roll
→ prop state
→ environmental reaction
→ transition mask
```

Lighthouse must use that exact causal chain. All action beats are progress windows, not one-time events, so reverse scrolling reconstructs the story precisely.

### Shared cast and recurring objects

| System | Purpose | Required states |
|---|---|---|
| Keeper | Hero silhouette and physical agency | idle, dock-walk, carry-book, page-hold, run, brace, swim, climb, turn-wheel, release |
| Keeping Book | The stakes made physical | closed, carried, opened, ink erasing, names recovered, names lifted |
| Sea | Antagonist and memory archive | harbor calm, unnaturally rising, storm wall, submerged current, clearing, dawn reflection |
| Fresnel lamp | Story machine and transition language | dim, stuttering, off-axis, assembling, aligned, full beam, bird release |
| Memory names | Evidence of the lost sailors | ink text, fibres, floating cards, gold light, birds |

### Shared material language

- salt-stained, fibrous paper for town, keeper clothing and wreck;
- wet translucent glass for the lantern room;
- watercolor water under calm light;
- black ink-water and torn foam under storm;
- warm brass, prismatic glass and gold dust in the lamp;
- peach paper, dry brush marks and feather-like strokes at dawn.

No generic low-poly plastic materials. Every material needs a paper, pigment, glass, water, or brass rationale.

## Chapter shot plans

## I. Harbor at Dusk — **Tend what you can**

**Dramatic function:** establish the keeper's ritual and the ordinary world worth protecting.

**Spatial design:** wide harbor foreground, diagonal dock leading to lighthouse headland, village layered behind the water.

**Shot progression:**

| Local progress | Camera | Keeper action | World response |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.18 | Low waterline establishing shot | Small keeper exits boathouse | tide reflection, gulls, lanterns waking |
| 0.18–0.46 | Lateral dock tracking shot | Walks with book/lantern, checks a mooring | boat settles; rope slackens; window lights reflect |
| 0.46–0.72 | Push past keeper toward headland | Notices distant dark wave | gulls leave; lights subtly dim; water loses warmth |
| 0.72–1.00 | Follow behind keeper climbing to lamp room | Carries book inside | first beam sweep becomes transition mask |

**Required set pieces:** layered dock, rope with real tension state, boat, boathouse threshold, walking keeper, village windows, gull flock.

**Pass condition:** it must feel calm enough that the reader recognizes the storm as a violation.

---

## II. The First Name — **Hold the page**

**Dramatic function:** turn an abstract premise into a personal loss.

**Spatial design:** circular lantern room, curved rain glass, desk, book, brass lens mechanism behind keeper.

**Shot progression:**

| Local progress | Camera | Keeper action | World response |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.20 | Travel through rain-streaked glass | Keeper places book on desk | exterior harbor refracts in the panes |
| 0.20–0.48 | Over-shoulder book close-up | Opens page to Elias Rune | paper fibres and ink texture become visible |
| 0.48–0.74 | Tight orbit around hands/page | Tries to hold the name | red-black ink shakes, separates, then lifts into wind |
| 0.74–1.00 | Pull back toward lens | Keeper reaches for the stuttering lamp | room darkens; first storm flash fills glass |

**Required interaction:** name erasure must be physically legible—ink threads detach from page, resist the keeper's hand, and travel toward the exterior storm.

**Pass condition:** the reader feels the keeper fail at a concrete action, not merely observes an animated book.

---

## III. The Storm Wall — **Hold the harbor**

**Dramatic function:** turn the keeper into an active protector under impossible scale.

**Spatial design:** exposed dock, boat at left foreground, lighthouse at right/mid distance, massive water wall beyond the harbor mouth.

**Shot progression:**

| Local progress | Camera | Keeper action | World response |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.22 | Handheld-feeling side track at dock level | Keeper races through rain | rain streaks, loose paper, windows black out |
| 0.22–0.52 | Low angle at boat cleat | Ties / pulls mooring rope | rope goes taut, boat slams, dock boards flex |
| 0.52–0.78 | Tilt and pull to reveal scale | Braces against wind and beam | storm wall advances; broken beam cuts spray |
| 0.78–1.00 | Water breach fills screen | Keeper is swept into sea / dives after names | white foam becomes underwater transition |

**Required interaction:** rope tension is driven by keeper reach; the boat and dock respond. The storm wall needs multiple depth layers, spray, foam, rain, and lightning—not a background plane.

**Pass condition:** this is the Lighthouse equivalent of Bao's chase: speed, danger, action, escalation.

---

## IV. What the Water Kept — **Recover what was taken**

**Dramatic function:** replace fear with awe and give each sailor a trace of personhood.

**Spatial design:** descending surface veil, wreck corridor, floating letters/bubbles, open negative space for a slow underwater camera.

**Shot progression:**

| Local progress | Camera | Keeper action | World response |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.18 | Break surface and sink | Keeper falls/swims down | sound and palette muffle; bubbles rise |
| 0.18–0.46 | Follow through tilted wreck | Swims with lantern | caustic shaft follows lantern; papers drift |
| 0.46–0.78 | Orbit individual memory objects | Touches/retrieves names | each card opens into a gold fibre/light, joins keeper |
| 0.78–1.00 | Camera turns upward | Keeper follows gathered lights | lights spiral toward lighthouse above |

**Required interaction:** only one to three meaningful memory reveals are needed, but each must feel touched/recovered—not all cards appearing as decoration.

**Pass condition:** a player can describe the chapter as a descent and rescue, not an underwater gallery.

---

## V. The Lamp Rekindled — **Make a way home**

**Dramatic function:** earned ascent and mechanical transformation.

**Spatial design:** interior spiral stair with occluding central column; ascending stairs; final intimate Fresnel lens chamber.

**Shot progression:**

| Local progress | Camera | Keeper action | World response |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.42 | Behind/above on spiral track | Climbs carrying recovered name-light | stairs turn beneath; storm flashes through slots |
| 0.42–0.65 | Close lens-chamber framing | Places names into lamp core | individual lamp rings begin to align |
| 0.65–0.85 | Slow circular orbit | Turns brass wheel | rings rotate, beam coheres, storm is cut open |
| 0.85–1.00 | Move through lens into sky | Keeper shields eyes | full beam becomes dawn transition |

**Required interaction:** the lamp must not simply become bright. The keeper inserts, turns, aligns, and releases; every action has a visible mechanical consequence.

**Pass condition:** this must become the project's 5/5 benchmark before any other chapter is polished.

---

## VI. Birds of Morning — **Let them go**

**Dramatic function:** resolve the central paradox: keeping names safe means returning them to life, not preserving them in a book.

**Spatial design:** lantern room initially, then broad restored harbor and coast-wide aerial retreat.

**Shot progression:**

| Local progress | Camera | Keeper action | World response |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.28 | Beside keeper and lens | Opens book / raises lantern | storm drains, first name-light forms bird |
| 0.28–0.62 | Beam-follow camera | Releases names | birds launch sequentially through glass; village lights return |
| 0.62–0.86 | Retreat through lens | Keeper watches | flock crosses foreground; tide settles; harbor wakes |
| 0.86–1.00 | High aerial final shot | Keeper remains small and still | dawn coast, birds disperse, final line |

**Pass condition:** birds must emerge because of keeper/lamp action, not appear as a decorative final layer.

## Technical implementation plan

### Phase A — shared systems first

1. Finish `Keeper` pose API and make hand/prop alignment stable.
2. Build `KeepingBook` as a page/ink-fibre system with a controllable name anchor.
3. Build `FresnelLamp` with separate glass, brass ring, core, beam, and name-particle groups.
4. Make `SeaSurface` quality-tier aware; add foam/spray fallback for low devices.
5. Replace generic `LighthouseScenes.js` with six scene modules. Shared objects can be factories, but each scene owns its geometry, camera path, and update logic.

### Phase B — chapter production order

1. **Lamp Rekindled** — establish the quality bar.
2. **Storm Wall** — establish movement, danger, and scale.
3. **First Name** — establish intimate prop interaction.
4. **Wreck Memories** — establish underwater material behavior.
5. **Harbor at Dusk** — establish believable everyday world.
6. **Birds of Morning** — assemble payoff from prior systems.

### Phase C — visual integration

- Prefer animated set pieces over static image plates.
- Keep generated artwork as distant art direction only; never use it as the primary shot.
- Add shader passes only where they articulate material: wet ink, sea, glass/refraction, caustics, beam dust.
- Drive all story changes from local scroll progress windows.

---

## Review loop

After every completed chapter, run these five independent review lenses:

1. **Film director:** Is the keeper's goal, obstacle, and consequence legible in three seconds?
2. **Character animator:** Is the keeper's silhouette readable and does body weight sell the action?
3. **Environment artist:** Does the setting react to the keeper, and is material behavior specific?
4. **Technical director:** Is reverse scrub exact, frame allocation stable, and low-tier fallback coherent?
5. **Accessibility reviewer:** Does transcript/copy convey the beat; can reduced-motion users still understand it?

A chapter advances only when every reviewer scores it at least 4/5. The Lamp chapter must reach 5/5 first and becomes the comparison standard.

## Definition of done

The experience is done only when:

- all six chapters have a distinct space, verb, camera grammar, and environmental reaction;
- the keeper is visible and purposeful throughout;
- the book, sea, lamp, and names form a continuous causal chain;
- all transitions are beam-led and reversible;
- backdrop art is supportive, never a replacement for animated action;
- desktop and mobile quality tiers remain coherent;
- the final dawn feels earned by prior actions.
